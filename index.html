Mineral Recon

Mineral recon is a game about a drone mining minerals on an asteroid. It was made for the ryan laley gamejam with a theme of "Contrast"

It was the first gamejam I took part in and I worked on it with my brother. It was created in 2 days and there are some problems but I am still proud of it.

The main idea we took from contrast was to make the visible light spectrum limited to a certain range that the player could adjust and then use that to hide hostile creatures in it.

The project was very fun to work on because the movement required implementing a 3D navigation for both the player and the creatures that hunt the player.

I made the 3D "navmesh" entirely guessing what I needed by creating a 3D grid of points and line tracing for paths between them. It worked surprisingly well for something I came up with quickly. The pathfinding works ok with it though the creatures do get stuck sometimes for other reasons. It does not scale very well however.

I also created the movement and the contrast mechanic for the game.

My brother created the UI, mining, the creatures other than path finding and guns. I really like how they turned out.

Souls of Tuonela

This is the second completed gamejam project. This was made with my brother and his friend.

The project is a fairly simple retro shooter with 3 weapons and a few enemies inspired by Finnish folklore.

I created the weapons and a few of the enemies mostly the swans and a bit of the gnomes. Also did a bit of level design for example the field and enemy placements.

It was fun to try and create interesting situations for the player to encounter and to create different behaviours for the hostile creatures.

I liked how I got the weapons to feel as well but I might have also made the shotgun a bit too overpowered initially. The weapons all have their own unique properties like the machinegun projectiles bounce and the shotgun projectiles go through walls.

Also wish that we could have done more with the kantele puzzles. We definitely used up all of the allowed time to make the game.

Behind the mask

The third gamejam project and the most recent one. For this one we had a couple of more friends joining us.

The idea was to create a kind of stealth game for escaping a castle. We made a lot of progress on designing the level and the enemies but we didn't really have a flow to the game until very late. The game ended up being a bit difficult because of this and it is also quite the maze.

I worked on the stealth mechanics and the mask. The stealth mechanics are fairly basic with nothing special about it. Shadows make you more hidden and moving slower makes you less likely to be heard. The mask was the part I am proud of.

The mask generally shows your status with the mask getting darker when hidden and getting cracked when health is running low. The movement of the player is also shown on it and some special conditions.

I also tried to do something interesting with the stabbing taking a faster step but it could use a bit more work.

Title

The idea for this mod is to allow for the 29th Infantry division community to build scenarios for our one life game style.

It has been a long running project that has been worked on occasionally with it being around 6 years now. Some others members have also worked on it but I have made a lot of the core features that get used almost every time. It is fairly configurable for what happens at what stage but the overhead for who is running the scenario is inteded to be only a few button presses for every round (usually from 10 to 15 minutes).

The challenge has been to work in the confines of the squad SDK that wants things to be done in certain ways but also does not expose some functions that would make developing more convenient. Another challenge was designing something that is meant to be easy to use but also to extend. I have done a few rewrites of the systems and recently I have been able to add things without feeling any pain from pre-existing code.

Some of the features are: Controlling objects and placing them but also configuring each one to respawn or disabling damage of some more specific features, Controlling round flow (automation would be possible but has been intentionally left out), And added some more interesting objectives.

Designing the interfaces for those has been interesting and has taught me a lot. The visuals are not ideal as I haven't gone out of my way to really work on them. Some of the more interesting objects have been the free form capture zone that can take the shape of a polygon and the movement restrictions that can be placed similarly. Another two are a laptop that needs to be carried to a goal and the movable weapons that I have added. These two required a bit of setup through the squad inventory system.

All of these have been required to work in multiplayer scenarios and have taught me a lot about networked games. Specifically in unreal.
